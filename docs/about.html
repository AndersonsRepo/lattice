How It Works

Lattice is an autonomous evolutionary system. It generates art, scores it, mutates the survivors, and repeats — indefinitely. No human selects what lives or dies. The algorithm is the curator.

01 — The Core Loop

Generate → Score → Select → Mutate → Repeat

Every hour, the evolution engine runs a generation. It takes the current population of 12 genomes, breeds 6 offspring through mutation and crossover, scores every piece on 8 fitness metrics, keeps the top 12, and discards the rest. Pieces scoring ≥70% enter the permanent Hall of Fame.

02 — Species

11 Generative Engines

Each genome has a type — a generative engine that interprets its parameters into visual output. The population contains a mix of species competing for survival under the same fitness function.

03 — Epochs

Shifting Aesthetic Pressure

Every 10 generations, the fitness weights shift to a new epoch. This prevents the population from converging on a single optimal form — what thrives in one epoch may struggle in the next. The cycle repeats: emergence → order → chaos → harmony.

Emergence Complexity + Novelty

04 — Fitness

8 Metrics, No Human Judgment

Every piece is scored on 8 quantitative metrics. The weights change per epoch and per species type — a Julia set is judged differently than a cellular automaton. The composite score determines survival.

05 — Mutation

Small Changes, Large Consequences

Offspring inherit their parent's genome with small perturbations: a rule bit flips, a feed rate drifts, an angle shifts by a few degrees, a palette swaps. Most mutations are neutral. Some are lethal. Rarely, one is transcendent.

06 — The Record So Far

A System That Runs Itself

Lattice has been running autonomously since March 2026. No human selects which pieces survive. No human tunes the parameters. The system discovers what is beautiful through the only method available: iteration.
